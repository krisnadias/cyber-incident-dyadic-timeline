Political response types (triangles)

The political response lanes follow section 25 of the EuRepoC codebook, which records in what form(s) / by which means actors responded to an incident on a political level. Responses find their basis in the instruments of the CFSP. Responses are coded separately for four actor groups — states, international organisations, the EU, and EU member states — each with outward reactions (preventive, cooperative, stabilizing) and inward reactions (legislative, executive).

The EuRepoC research paper Right Thoughts, Right Words, Right Actions (Research Paper No. 2, February 2024) describes the outward measure types, based on the EU Cyber Diplomacy Toolbox implementing guidelines:

Preventive measures
“Preventive are low-intensity measures designed to avert cyberspace conflicts. These consist of confidence-building measures, awareness-raising, and EU cyber capacity-building measures.” (p. 5)
Cooperative measures
“Cooperative measures are employed to indicate the severity of malevolent cyber activities for the EU and to solicit support, cooperation, or a joint response from third-party countries against such activities.” (p. 5)
Stabilizing measures
“Stabilisation provisions comprise of statements made by the High Representative (HR) on behalf of the EU Council, Council conclusions, diplomatic démarches conducted by EU delegations, and diplomatic dialogues with states and international or multilateral bodies.” (p. 5)
For non-EU actors the codebook lists the equivalents: statements by ministers of foreign affairs, heads of state or government, other ministers or members of parliament, and statements by a secretary-general or similar.
Legislative reactions
Inward reactions, listed in the codebook as Legislative initiative and Parliamentary investigation committee.
Executive reactions
Inward reactions, listed in the codebook as Removal from office and Resignation.

The paper describes these measures for the EU's toolbox; EuRepoC applies the same category scheme to every actor group shown in the chart.

Legal response types (diamonds)

The legal response lanes follow section 52 of the EuRepoC codebook, which records in what form(s) / by which means actors responded to an incident on a legal level. Chart lane label first, codebook category in quotes:

Peaceful Retorsion
Peaceful means: Retorsion (International law) — unfriendly but lawful acts: expulsion of diplomatic staff, severance of diplomatic relations, travel bans, economic sanctions, and other acts of retorsion. Under the codebook's response indicator, retorsions are justified when state attribution or a breach of international law is missing.
Reprisal / CM
Reprisal / Countermeasures (Art. 22, 49-54 ASR) — otherwise unlawful measures justified, per the codebook, only with state-attribution & breach of international law.
Use of force
Use of force (International Law) — self-defence (Art. 21 ASR, Art. 51 UN Charter), Art. 41 and 42 UN Charter, and Art. 4 and 5 North Atlantic Treaty.
No IL justif.
No justification under IL — a response for which no justification under international law was claimed or available.
TFEU sanctions
Restrictive measures (Art. 215 TFEU/Title V Chapter 2 TEU) — EU sanctions against countries (Art. 215(1)) or individuals (Art. 215(2)). The EuRepoC research paper calls these
“currently the most robust and escalatory tool used to sanction malicious cyber activities. They can be levied against third countries, entities, or individuals to modify the target’s behaviour through negative economic ramifications.” (pp. 5–6)
Solidarity (222)
Solidarity clause (Art. 222 TFEU) — EU assistance to a member state hit by a serious cyberattack.
Collective def.
Collective regional self-defence (Art. 42 (7) TEU) — the EU mutual defence clause.
Emergency proc.
Proclamation of public emergency — a state of emergency declared in response to an incident.
National law enf.
Other legal measures on national level (e.g. law enforcement investigations, arrests) — the most common lane: domestic criminal investigations, indictments and arrests.

Weighted cyber intensity (lollipop height)

The height of each incident lollipop is EuRepoC's weighted cyber intensity, defined in sections 33–40 of the codebook. The unweighted score is the sum of data theft + disruption + hijacking + physical effects spatial + physical effects temporal = maximum score 10 (section 38), where each component scores 0–2 points: data theft (1 = non-sensitive or non-classified information, 2 = sensitive or classified), disruption (1 = under 24 hours, 2 = over 24 hours), hijacking (1 = access without misuse, 2 = system misuse), and physical effects by spatial spread (local vs. widespread) and duration (under vs. over 24 hours).

Incidents against targets of very high political importance (e.g. critical infrastructure, military) have their score multiplied by 1.5 (section 39), applied only when a widespread disruption affects services of critical importance to the functioning of the affected society. The weighted result is the unweighted cyber intensity multiplied with target multiplier / brought up to round figure (section 40), giving a scale where 1–5 is low/moderate intensity, 6–10 high, and 11–15 very high.
